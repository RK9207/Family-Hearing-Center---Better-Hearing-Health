# Content direction

Family Hearing Practice · Better Hearing Health · Flower Mound, TX

## Core message

The primary value proposition is a local hearing evaluation with an audiologist who reviews the results during the visit and discusses what to do next. The primary visitor outcome is clarity about their hearing and a practical next step. More confident participation in everyday conversations is a meaningful longer-term goal when appropriate care helps.

Use the [project brief](LANDING_PAGE_PROJECT_BRIEF_-_FAMILY_HEARING_PRACTICE.md) for campaign inputs, [research](RESEARCH.md) for approved source evidence and review transcriptions, and the [page strategy](PAGE_STRATEGY.md) for sequence. Actual primary and secondary campaign keywords, ad language, and an offer have yet to be supplied. The [keyword research](KEYWORD_RESEARCH.md) records unvalidated candidates. The approved theme is local audiology for hearing evaluation in Flower Mound. Hearing aid fitting and support for existing devices, including adjustments or repair as appropriate, are supporting service themes. Helping a parent is a supporting audience need.

## Hierarchy and tone

| Level | What earns the space |
|---|---|
| H1 and hero | Name hearing evaluations and Flower Mound. Follow with the patient benefit of reviewing results during the visit and understanding the next step. Identify the audiology practice and mention hearing aid care briefly. |
| Major section headings | Answer the visitor’s next question. Emphasize signs worth checking, what happens during an evaluation, and support for new or existing hearing aids. Give the practice, reviews, directions, and conversion area clear labels. |
| Short supporting copy | Timing, the painless nature of the test, same-visit discussion, the founder’s two relevant qualifications, and fitting and follow-up details. |
| Callout or card | Give existing hearing aid wearers one compact callout. Keep review cards for distinct patient experiences. Express the visit as a short sequence. |
| FAQ | Cover remaining questions about whether hearing aids may be recommended, existing devices, visit logistics, and confirmed cost or coverage information. |

Use a calm, welcoming voice. Say “hearing test” when the reader will benefit from the familiar term, and use “evaluation” where it conveys that the visit includes discussing results and next steps. Address the visitor directly, with one or two mentions of helping a parent or loved one where relevant. Keep paragraphs short and use concrete examples. Introduce necessary clinical terms in plain language. Use the “Better Hearing Health” theme through the page’s benefit language. Service, city, and action carry the first-screen message.

## Translate services into outcomes

| Source-backed fact | Practical benefit | Meaningful outcome to convey |
|---|---|---|
| Results reviewed during the visit | The visitor can ask questions and understand the audiologist’s recommendation while there. | Leave with a clearer sense of what to do next. |
| Evaluation is painless and typically takes 30–60 minutes, depending on tests | The visitor knows what to plan for and has fewer unknowns about the visit. | It feels manageable to schedule the appointment or help a relative attend. |
| Personalized hearing aid fitting and programming | Settings and fit can be tailored to where the visitor struggles. | When appropriate, hearing aids may make conversation easier in everyday settings. |
| Practice works with hearing aids patients already own | The audiologist can assess whether adjustments or other support may help. | The visitor has a path to address discomfort or listening difficulties with their current devices. |
| Follow-up and education | Visitors can ask for help as their needs change and learn to use their devices. | They can feel more comfortable using the devices in daily life. |

Use daily situations such as listening across a dinner table or following a conversation in a noisy room. Present outcomes as possibilities and patient stories as individual experiences. Avoid a universal outcome, guaranteed improvement, or a claim that a test will lead to a particular device.

## Trust and objections

- **Hero badge.** Use the Flower Mound Google Business Profile, with 4.9 stars and 655 reviews documented in the supplied screenshots. Check the current rating and count before publication.
- **Clinical credibility.** Use Dr. Allison Liberio’s owner and founder role, prior DFW ENT practice experience, and UNT audiology teaching. Keep credentials practice-level until the Flower Mound roster is confirmed. Use genuine, appropriately licensed photography.
- **Dedicated review section.** Start with Peter Taurchini for Flower Mound and clear results, Deb Bean for patience with her mother and existing-device adjustments, and Shannon Primer for an easy booking and test experience. Preserve original wording and meaning in any excerpts, attribute each quote, omit stale relative dates, and link to the supplied local Google reviews destination.
- **Hesitation about hearing aids.** Explain that the audiologist reviews results and discusses options based on the findings. The evaluation has value for people who are unsure about hearing aids or already use them.
- **Practical uncertainty.** Explain visit length, comfort, and same-visit results in the process section. Use the FAQ for direct answers people may look for before committing. Avoid pricing, coverage, financing, or availability claims without confirmation. If pricing or coverage stays open, give visitors a confirmed contact route to ask the clinic.

## CTA and form copy

- **Book Appointment** is the primary button label throughout. It opens the booking widget. Nearby copy should tell the visitor what they can book and why it’s worth doing. Keep the ad’s action and the first screen consistent once the actual ads are supplied.
- **Call or Text [confirmed number]** is the secondary button label from the brief. Confirm the campaign number and SMS capability before publishing it. The brief’s number ends in 994-4084 and the clinic site’s number ends in 997-4084.
- **Read all our Google reviews** and **Get Directions** are supporting actions. Give them a secondary visual weight.
- **Request an Appointment** introduces the separate inline form. A single line should tell visitors that the practice will follow up about scheduling. Describe the result of submission as a request. Use the form’s actual behavior to guide success and error copy. Add a reply-time statement only if confirmed by the practice.
- **Closing CTA.** Bring the visitor back to the value of getting their hearing checked and discussing the results in Flower Mound. Lead with the booking button and then introduce the request form for people who would like the practice to follow up.
