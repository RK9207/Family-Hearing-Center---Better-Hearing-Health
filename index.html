Signs it may be time for a hearing evaluation

Hearing usually changes gradually, so early signs are easy to miss or explain away. If any of these sound familiar, an evaluation can show what’s going on.

Sometimes the people around you notice first. If a spouse, friend, or adult child has mentioned the TV volume or missed conversations, it’s worth taking seriously. The same applies if you’re noticing these changes in a parent.

  • You often ask people to repeat themselves
  • Conversations are hard to follow in restaurants and other noisy places
  • Others say the TV or radio is louder than they’d like
  • Phone calls are harder to hear clearly
  • You notice ringing or buzzing in your ears
  • It seems like people mumble more than they used to
  • You skip social plans because listening takes so much effort
  • Hearing loss runs in your family

These experiences are reasons to consider an evaluation, not a diagnosis. Some hearing concerns need medical attention rather than hearing aids, and an evaluation helps determine which.

What happens at your hearing evaluation

Knowing what to expect makes the appointment easier to schedule, whether it’s for you or for someone you’re helping.

  1. 1

    Step 1: Share what you’ve noticed

    Your audiologist starts by asking about your hearing concerns, the situations where listening is hardest, and what matters to you day to day. That context comes first, before any recommendation.

    Your goals guide the visit
  2. 2

    Step 2: Complete a painless set of hearing tests

    Safe, non-invasive tests measure how well you hear tones and understand speech, and check how your middle ear is working.

    Typically 30–60 minutes, depending on the tests
    What the tests check
    Tympanometry
    Checks how your eardrum responds to gentle changes in air pressure, which can reveal middle-ear issues such as fluid behind the eardrum.
    Pure-tone audiometry
    Finds the softest tones you can hear across a range of pitches.
    Speech recognition threshold
    Measures the softest level at which you can hear and repeat simple words.
    Word recognition testing
    Measures how clearly you understand words at a comfortable volume. Results help predict how much you may benefit from hearing aids.
  3. 3

    Step 3: Go over your results together

    Your audiologist reviews the results with you during the same appointment, explains what they mean, and discusses next steps. Hearing aids may be one option; some concerns call for medical attention instead, and the evaluation helps determine which.

    Results reviewed the same visit

Support for new hearing aids, and the ones you already wear

If your results show that hearing aids may help, your audiologist guides you through the options and stays involved after the fitting. The aim is a device that suits your hearing, your lifestyle, and the places where listening is hardest for you.

  1. A recommendation based on your evaluation

    Your hearing results and lifestyle needs guide which styles and features make sense, from behind-the-ear and receiver-in-canal designs to custom in-the-ear and in-the-canal models.

  2. Personalized fitting and programming

    Settings are tailored to your hearing so your devices are set up around your needs from the start.

  3. Adjustments as your needs change

    Follow-up fine-tuning helps with comfort and sound as you get used to the devices and as your hearing changes over time.

  4. Cleaning, troubleshooting, and repairs

    Hearing aids need regular care. Ongoing maintenance helps keep yours working the way they should.

  5. Education and ongoing support

    Learn how to use and care for your devices, including features such as rechargeable batteries and smartphone controls.

Depending on your hearing and the device, modern hearing aids can offer:

  • Clearer speech in conversation
  • Better hearing in noisy places
  • Wireless connection to phones and TVs
  • Less listening fatigue
  • Rechargeable options
  • Comfortable, discreet designs
  • Settings for different environments

Hearing aids don’t cure hearing loss, but they can make hearing and communication easier. With proper care, most last about four to six years.

Already wear hearing aids?

We work with patients who already have hearing aids. Book a visit if yours feel uncomfortable, aren’t helping the way they used to, or need cleaning or repair. Regular hearing evaluations also track changes in your hearing so your devices can keep up.

Questions before you book

Will my evaluation automatically lead to hearing aids?

No. Your audiologist reviews your results with you and discusses options based on what the evaluation shows. Hearing aids may be recommended when they’re likely to help. Some hearing concerns need medical attention rather than hearing aids, and the evaluation helps determine which.

Can you help with hearing aids I already have?

Yes. We work with patients who already have hearing aids. Your audiologist can check how they’re working for you and help with adjustments, cleaning, troubleshooting, or repairs as needed.

How much does an evaluation cost, and do you accept insurance?

Costs and insurance coverage vary from patient to patient. If you’d like to understand your coverage before your visit, call our Flower Mound office at (817) 994-4084 and ask about your plan.

How long does a hearing evaluation take?

Most evaluations take 30–60 minutes, depending on which tests you need.

Is the hearing test painful?

No. Hearing tests are safe, non-invasive, and painless.

Will I get my results the same day?

Yes. Your audiologist reviews your results during the same appointment and talks through next steps with you.

Do I still need a hearing test if I already wear hearing aids?

Yes. Regular evaluations help monitor changes in your hearing so your hearing aids can keep working well for you.

Will hearing aids restore my hearing completely?

Hearing aids don’t cure hearing loss, but they can significantly improve hearing and communication. With proper care, most hearing aids last about four to six years.

Are hearing aids hard to use?

Modern hearing aids are designed to be user-friendly, and many offer rechargeable batteries and smartphone controls. Your audiologist shows you how to use and care for your devices and provides ongoing support.

Can you help with ringing in my ears?

Tinnitus, the ringing or buzzing some people hear, is one of the concerns the practice helps patients with. Mention it when you share your concerns at the start of your visit.

Do you see children?

Yes. Family Hearing Practice cares for patients of every age, from newborns to older adults. Children’s evaluations use age-appropriate tests in a child-friendly setting.

What are your Flower Mound office hours?

Monday through Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM. We’re also open the first Saturday of each month and closed on Sundays.
