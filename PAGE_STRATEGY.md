# Page strategy

Family Hearing Practice · Better Hearing Health · Flower Mound, TX

## Objective and campaign intent

Generate booked hearing evaluations at the Flower Mound practice. The primary conversion is a completed booking through the supplied MyHearingPortal widget. The inline appointment-request form and phone contact give visitors other ways to reach the practice. A widget open and an appointment request are distinct from a completed booking.

The approved intent is finding a local audiologist for a hearing evaluation in Flower Mound. “Audiologist Flower Mound” and “hearing test Flower Mound” are unvalidated candidates. The [keyword research](KEYWORD_RESEARCH.md) contains discovery hypotheses. The [project brief](LANDING_PAGE_PROJECT_BRIEF_-_FAMILY_HEARING_PRACTICE.md) supplies the city, the service topic, and the booking action. Campaign keywords, ads, and any promotional offer are pending.

The main visitor question is “Can I get my hearing checked by a local audiologist who will explain what the results mean?” The first screen should answer with the service, location, same-visit results, local review proof, and **Book Appointment**. The evaluation itself is the appointment being offered. When live ads are available, compare their service, location, promise, and CTA with the first screen and booking widget. A discount or free evaluation requires explicit clinic and campaign confirmation.

Hearing aid fitting and existing-device support can share this page as supporting needs. Ads centered on repair, tinnitus, children’s testing, or hearing protection call for a destination that answers that specific need. Roanoke traffic calls for a Roanoke destination.

## Visitor and conversion journey

The primary audience includes adults noticing a change in their hearing, adults seeking help with existing aids, and family members helping an older relative. Some will recognize the problem immediately. Others need to see what the visit involves and whether the practice treats patients with patience before they act.

Use a medium-length page with short sections. Prompt action when the visitor first recognizes the service, after they understand the visit, and after logistics and objections have been addressed. The Google rating establishes early trust. The founder’s credentials and specific patient experiences supply deeper proof farther down. Keep **Book Appointment** prominent in the sticky header. Desktop navigation should anchor to the evaluation, reviews, FAQ, and location. Mobile needs the logo and booking button in the header plus a secondary sticky phone action.

## Section sequence

### 1. Hero

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Confirm the search match and let ready visitors act. “Is this a hearing evaluation in Flower Mound, and can I book it here?”
- **Message and content.** A local audiologist can evaluate your hearing, discuss the results during the visit, and explain next steps. Name Family Hearing Practice, mention hearing aid care briefly, show the Google rating and count, and offer **Book Appointment**.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Replace uncertainty with a specific next step. Use the Flower Mound Google listing as the first trust signal.
- **Visual and transitions.** Continue the service and location expectation from the ad or search. Use one evaluation image to make the service tangible. On mobile, the message, CTA, and proof precede the image. The next section helps the visitor recognize their own reason to book.

### 2. Signs it may be time for an evaluation

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Help visitors decide whether the service is relevant. “Do the changes I’m noticing warrant a hearing check?”
- **Message and content.** Use a few familiar experiences such as missing words in noisy rooms, asking for repeats, or turning up the TV. Include a brief acknowledgment of family members noticing changes. These are reasons to consider an evaluation.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Normalize the experience with respectful, specific language grounded in the clinic’s hearing test guidance. Avoid diagnosing from a symptom list.
- **Visual and transitions.** Follow the hero with a short, text-led section and no standalone CTA. The examples lead directly into what a visit can clarify.

### 3. What happens at the evaluation

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Remove uncertainty and prompt booking. “Will it hurt, how long will it take, and when will I get answers?”
- **Message and content.** Describe discussing concerns, completing a painless hearing evaluation that typically takes 30–60 minutes depending on the tests, and talking through results and next steps during the visit. Repeat **Book Appointment** here.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Give visitors a predictable experience and a reason to act even if they are unsure whether they need hearing aids.
- **Visual and transitions.** Use a compact three-step sequence, with timing and same-visit results easy to spot. Move from recognizing a problem to understanding it, then explain the support available if hearing aids are appropriate or already in use.

### 4. Hearing aid care

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Show the scope of support and keep existing wearers in the journey. “Can you help me decide what I need or adjust what I already have?”
- **Message and content.** Lead with personalized selection, fitting, and follow-up, then make help with existing devices easy to find. Connect adjustments to goals such as comfort and following conversation. Keep the benefits conditional on the person and their needs.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Make hearing aid care feel like ongoing support and a discussion about options. Ground reassurance in the practice’s published services.
- **Visual and transitions.** Use a compact editorial block with a distinct line for existing wearers. A device or fitting photo is optional if it clarifies the service. The sticky header carries the booking action. The next section introduces the practice behind the care.

### 5. Owner-led practice

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Establish who is responsible for the care. “Who leads this practice, and what experience do they bring?”
- **Message and content.** Introduce founder and audiologist Dr. Allison Liberio. Her five years at a DFW ENT practice and audiology teaching at the University of North Texas are the two most useful details. Describe her as the founder. Confirm the Flower Mound roster before stating who sees patients there.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Give the practice a human face and substantiate its expertise.
- **Visual and transitions.** Use a genuine founder portrait and a short profile. The service section explains the care, the profile establishes credibility, and reviews show how patients experienced it. Use the persistent booking action.

### 6. Google reviews

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Show credible patient experiences. “Do people here feel listened to and understand their options?”
- **Message and content.** Lead with Peter Taurchini on explanations at Flower Mound, Deb Bean on patient care with her mother, and Shannon Primer on booking and the test experience. These are the first three reviews on desktop and mobile. Display the local Google rating and review count, with **Read all our Google reviews**.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Reinforce clarity, respect, and ease through actual patient accounts. Preserve authorship and meaning. Individual experiences should remain attributable to the reviewer.
- **Visual and transitions.** Give reviews a dedicated section with readable text and a quieter background. The research screenshots show 4.9 stars from 655 reviews. Check the Flower Mound listing before publication. Show up to six reviews on desktop and up to three on mobile. Move from patient proof to specific objections.

### 7. FAQ

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Resolve the last practical questions before booking. “Will they recommend hearing aids automatically? Can they help with my current aids? What will this cost?”
- **Message and content.** Prioritize whether every evaluation leads to hearing aids, whether the practice can help with existing aids, and timing, comfort, or results for visitors who skipped the walkthrough. Use confirmed pricing or coverage facts only. If needed, direct visitors to the practice to ask about costs before booking once the contact route is confirmed.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Reduce pressure with short answers and plain explanations of what the evaluation can clarify.
- **Visual and transitions.** Use one narrow accordion column. No image or section-specific booking button is needed. After the reviews demonstrate the experience, the FAQ clears practical objections and the map answers where to go.

### 8. Flower Mound location and directions

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Confirm proximity and arrival details. “Where is the office, and how do I get there?”
- **Message and content.** Show 4491 Long Prairie Rd #400, Flower Mound, TX 75028, clinic hours supported by the current site, the supplied map, and **Get Directions** with a location icon.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Help people picture the trip and verify they’re booking at the right location.
- **Visual and transitions.** Put the address before the map and directions directly below it. A genuine Flower Mound exterior photo can help visitors recognize the entrance if supplied. Keep the map as the primary visual otherwise. Move from logistics to the final decision.

### 9. Booking and appointment request

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Convert visitors who are ready and give those who want help a way to reach the practice. “How do I book, and what happens if I send a request?”
- **Message and content.** Restate the benefit of getting answers about hearing in Flower Mound and present **Book Appointment** as the primary action. Introduce the separate **Request an Appointment** form right above its embed. Explain that submitting it asks the practice to follow up.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Give both actions clear expectations. The practice should confirm any response-time promise before it appears.
- **Visual and transitions.** The closing booking panel is the strongest visual moment after the hero. Give the form a quieter treatment with clear space after the location section. Leave sufficient clearance for the mobile sticky contact control.

### Footer

- **Purpose and visitor question.** Supply business and policy details after the conversion area. “Where can I check the practice’s policies or visit its main site?”
- **Message and content.** Include Privacy Policy, Visit Our Website, the Flower Mound contact details once confirmed, and the brief’s copyright treatment.
- **Emotional and trust role.** Make the clinic identity and policy easy to verify.
- **Visual and transitions.** Use a compact, low-density closing band with no additional sales emphasis. It follows the request form and ends the page.
